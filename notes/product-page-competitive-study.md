# Product Page Competitive Study

How AI-native products structure their product/homepage pages, and what to steal for Chronilogix.

Pages fetched live via WebFetch (June 2026). Three pages could not be retrieved (noted inline).

## Company-by-company findings

### Healthcare / clinical AI

#### Abridge — abridge.com
Section order: Hero ("Intelligence at the point of conversation") → customer logo wall → press mentions → outcome metrics ("78% decrease in cognitive load" tied to named institutions) → 3-column product split by user persona (Clinicians / Revenue Cycle / Nurses) → "Contextual Reasoning Engine" (how-it-works, IP-protected) → enterprise capabilities grid → Epic integration highlight → awards (KLAS, TIME, Fast Company) → mission/team ("practicing MDs, AI scientists") → press feed → footer.
Rhetoric: institutional credibility front-loaded. No demo video. Trust framed as compliance + clinical utility, not safety disclaimers. Named-institution outcomes are the proof spine.

#### Hippocratic AI — hippocraticai.com
Section order: Hero with explicit safety disclaimer ("do not diagnose or prescribe") → 4 de-identified audio call samples ("180M+ clinical interactions") → partner logo grid → 1000+ agent catalog (filterable) → "5.0T+ parameter constellation architecture" → clinical validation ("7,500 US-licensed clinicians, 725K test calls") → executive testimonials → outcome metrics → press → $404M funding → newsletter.
Rhetoric: safety-as-feature is the opening move. The de-identified audio samples are the single most distinctive choice in the set — they show the agent in action without showing UI. Clinical validation is presented as a quantified harm-reduction matrix, not a vague "we care about safety" blurb.

#### Suki — suki.ai
Section order: Hero ("The intelligence layer…") → ambient documentation feature deep dive → adaptability (100+ specialties, all major EHRs) → dual segmentation (Clinicians / Partners) → "Built different" 5-pillar differentiation → "400+ leading healthcare systems" logo wall → quantified results → testimonial → final CTA.
Rhetoric: positions AI as enabling clinician autonomy rather than replacing it ("practice medicine the way you want"). Built-different section is unusually direct competitive framing for healthcare.

#### Nabla — nabla.com
Section order: NEJM "featured in" badge (above hero) → Hero ("Smarter clinical workflows. Elevated care.") → "Deployed in 150+ health organizations" → 3 outcome metrics → testimonial carousel ("Loved by 85,000+ clinicians") → 40+ specialties → multilingual feature → customization → product overview → EHR integration → healthcare leader testimonials → EHR logos → care settings → trust/compliance ("we don't train on your data, no audio stored by default") → press → footer.
Rhetoric: NEJM badge above the fold is a strong move. Trust block uses concrete, falsifiable statements ("don't train models on your data," "no audio stored by default") instead of generic security bullets.

#### Commure — commure.com
Section order: Press banner ($70M raise, $7B valuation) → product nav grid → Hero with metric-heavy positioning (40M+ appointments, $25B+ claims, 150+ health systems) → "One Data Model" integrated platform diagram → 3-stage workflow (Front End / Mid Cycle / Back End) → customer logos → 7 named case studies with specific metrics → blog → press → events → CTA.
Rhetoric: workflow-stage mapping (Front/Mid/Back End) is the structural spine. Every testimonial pairs an endorsement with a quantified outcome — no decorative quotes.

#### Spring Health — springhealth.com
Section order: Hero ("Life gets overwhelming. Support should be simple.") → "20M covered lives" logo wall → 3 differentiation pillars (Right care right away / Tech that gets smarter / Partnership proven in outcomes) → workplace segmentation (individuals / managers / leaders) → "Proven clinical impact" with JAMA + CARF validation → connected platform overview → financial impact charts (Validation Institute citation) → named customer case studies ("15x increase in utilization") → resources → final CTA → footer.
Rhetoric: clinical validation (JAMA, CARF, Validation Institute) is given the same visual weight as customer logos. Mental health is framed as preventive medical spend reduction — explicit ROI story for buyers.

#### Lyra Health — lyrahealth.com
Section order: Hero ("Mental health care for all of life's moments") → employer logos → 5 core capabilities (with embedded stats — "30K+ providers, 100K hours annual clinical oversight," "$4,800 annual savings per employee") → audience segmentation (employers / providers / health systems) → outcomes ("2x faster recoveries, 81% maintained gains at 12 months") → video customer stories → resources/research → FAQ → final CTA.
Rhetoric: stats embedded inside capability cards rather than ghettoized into a metric strip. Equity language ("equitable outcomes across diverse racial and ethnic groups") appears as a first-class capability.

#### Woebot Health — woebothealth.com
Section order: Nav → Hero ("Mental health needs have multiplied. Support hasn't.") → award row (Newsweek, TIME100 AI) → problem statement (therapist shortage) → philosophy of care (founder credentials, "empathy and rigor") → value prop ("the ally that's with you through it all") → B2B2C positioning → certification badges (HIPAA, DTA) → footer.
Rhetoric: thinner page than peers. Heavy emphasis on founder clinical credentials as the trust anchor. Crisis/safety links are present but deferred to interior pages — they don't try to handle 988 on the homepage.

### Best-in-class B2B AI

#### Harvey — harvey.ai
Section order: Hero ("Practice Made Perfect") with customer logos directly underneath → "AI designed for legal and professional services" → 8 use cases (named, unexplained) → 3 executive testimonials → video gallery ("Real impact for real clients") → metrics dashboard (large numbers, low context) → enterprise security badges (SOC2 II, GDPR, ISO 27001, SAML SSO, audit logs) → final CTA ("Unlock Professional Class AI").
Rhetoric: trust without transparency — metrics shown without methodology, use cases listed without workflow detail. Security listed before capabilities. Sequence is logos → quotes → videos → metrics → security, layering credibility before the ask.

#### Glean — glean.com
Section order: Nav → Hero ("Work AI that understands your company") → 20+ enterprise logos → event banner → problem-solution ("AI costs are accelerating faster") → cost-efficiency framing (Better answers → Fewer tokens → Lower cost) → "Built for enterprise from day one" (ISO 42001, HIPAA, SOC 2, GDPR) → 6 stat block (35+ LLMs, 30% token reduction, 110 hours saved, 93% adoption, <6 months to ROI) → 5 named case studies → executive testimonials → 4-card platform architecture → Work AI Institute → support tiles → content feed → "Ask AI for a summary about Glean" (cross-LLM demo) → footer.
Rhetoric: cost-efficiency as a differentiator is unusual — they're competing on AI economics, not just outcomes. Inviting visitors to query ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini/Perplexity *about* Glean is a confident inversion of the demo pattern.

#### Sierra — sierra.ai
Section order: Hero ("Better customer experiences. Built on Sierra.") → customer logos → 4 feature benefits → executive testimonials → "Sierra Agent OS" how-it-works (Ghostwriter agent-builder, SOP upload) → "Use AI to improve your AI" (Insights: Explorer, Monitors, Experiments, Observability) → Agent Data Platform (memory + warehouse integrations) → trust/compliance badges (SOC 2, ISO 27001, ISO 42001, HIPAA, GDPR, EU AI Act) → CTA.
Rhetoric: observability-first. Heavy emphasis on showing decision traces and supervisor actions to defuse black-box concerns. EU AI Act badge is a forward-looking trust signal most peers don't carry yet.

#### Decagon — decagon.ai
Section order: Hero ("The AI concierge for every customer") with 3 dialogue snippets baked in → logo wall → testimonial carousel with metrics ("70% chat and voice resolution") → "The Decagon Difference" (Agent Operating Procedures in natural language) → Build/Optimize/Scale framework → omnichannel architecture (Voice/Chat/Email tabs) → 6-customer metrics row (65% cost reduction, 80% deflection) → research hub.
Rhetoric: opens with the agent literally speaking. "Move past complex configuration languages" is a direct shot at competitors framed as a user benefit.

#### Cursor — cursor.com
Section order: Hero ("Built to make you extraordinarily productive…") → Cursor Desktop demo with visible agent states (Reading docs, Generating plan) → company mission → 2022-2026 research timeline → agent task example ("Build landing page") → "Agents turn ideas into code" → Mission Control feature → autonomous parallel execution → omnichannel (Terminal, Slack, GitHub) → tab autocomplete → "over half of the Fortune 500" → testimonials (autonomy slider) → multi-model selection (OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini) → codebase understanding → changelog → team/careers → blog → CTA.

Rhetoric: artifact-first. The proof is the output, not the description. Reasoning steps are shown explicitly (checkmarks, file counts, search counts) to make the model auditable.

## Synthesis

### The 3 most common section archetypes (appear in ~every page)

1. **Compressed credibility stack right under the hero.** Logos → press mention → headline metric. No exceptions across the 13 accessible pages. The hero never stands alone; it is always immediately corroborated within one scroll.
2. **Quantified outcomes tied to named customers.** Generic stats are dying. Every strong page (Abridge, Nabla, Commure, Spring, Lyra, Glean, Decagon) pairs a percentage with a specific organization or population. The weakest example (Suki) currently shows placeholder zeros, which reads as a tell.
3. **Trust/compliance block before the final CTA.** Compliance badges + concrete data-handling statements ("we don't train on your data," "no audio stored by default") consistently appear in the bottom third. Healthcare pages treat this as load-bearing; B2B-AI pages treat it as table stakes but still surface it.

### Two more archetypes that are common but optional

4. **A "how it works" section that explains the architecture without revealing IP.** Abridge's "Contextual Reasoning Engine," Hippocratic's "5.0T+ constellation," Sierra's "Agent OS," Decagon's "Agent Operating Procedures." These give technical buyers a label to grab onto without exposing anything proprietary.
5. **A research/clinical authority anchor.** Above the fold (Nabla's NEJM badge) or as a dedicated callout (Spring's JAMA citation, Woebot's founder credentials). Always third-party — not internal claims.

### Patterns specific to healthcare AI (vs. general B2B AI)

- **Safety stance is stated, not implied.** Hippocratic literally says "do not diagnose or prescribe" in the hero. Nabla puts data-handling promises in plain English. General B2B AI pages can rely on SOC 2 badges; healthcare cannot.
- **Clinical validation appears as quantified harm reduction, not "we care about safety."** Hippocratic's 725K test calls, Spring's JAMA + CARF, Lyra's 100K hours of clinical oversight. The number itself is the trust signal.
- **Outcomes are framed as preventive cost.** Spring and Lyra both frame behavioral health investment as medical-spend reduction. This is the buyer's language, not the user's.
- **Named clinicians > anonymous quotes.** Woebot leans on its founder's credentials. Spring cites named studies. Anonymous patient quotes are absent across the healthcare set.
- **Crisis/988 protocols are linked, not solved on the homepage.** Woebot defers to interior pages. None of the surveyed pages try to handle crisis copy in the main flow — they signal it exists and move on.

### Patterns specific to general B2B AI (less applicable to Chronilogix)

- Agent state visualization on the page itself (Cursor, Decagon dialogue snippets, Sierra observability). Healthcare buyers want outcomes, not internal traces.
- Cost/token economics framing (Glean). Not a buyer concern in PMPM/subscription healthcare deals.
- Multi-model agnosticism (Cursor, Glean). Healthcare buyers want one accountable system, not a router.

### Anti-patterns to avoid (high credibility risk in healthcare)

- **Placeholder metrics.** Suki currently shows "0%" cards. Better to omit than ship empty.
- **Anonymous testimonials.** Already excluded by the brief, but worth restating — no healthcare buyer trusts an unattributed quote.
- **"Game-changer" / "revolutionary" superlatives without numbers.** Decagon and Harvey get away with this because their categories are entertainment-adjacent (concierge, legal). Behavioral health cannot.
- **Demo videos as the primary proof.** Abridge deliberately avoids embedded demo video; it relies on institutional outcomes instead. A glossy product video in a clinical context reads as marketing, not evidence.
- **Hero that opens with the AI feature.** Spring opens with the user's emotional state. Lyra opens with life's moments. Hippocratic leads with safety. Leading with "our AI does X" reads as vendor-first.
- **Burying compliance below the fold of the trust section.** The Aetna-quality buyer scans for SOC 2 / HIPAA / GDPR in the first 5 seconds of the trust block. Don't make them hunt.
- **Treating the AI as the subject of the sentence.** Across all healthcare pages, the platform brand is the subject. Rooney AI must not become the subject of Chronilogix's product page.

## Three candidate story arcs for the Chronilogix product page

Each ends with a Book-a-Demo CTA. All keep Chronilogix as the subject and Rooney AI as the engine.

### Arc A — "Credibility-stacked" (closest to Abridge / Spring Health)
Best for: a conference launch audience that will scan, not read.

1. Hero — Chronilogix platform in motion (Marcus chat loop) with the Resnicow eyebrow
2. Credibility bar — Aetna quote + 3 stats + compliance badges (already built)
3. Clinical Proof — Resnicow callout + 3 evidence cards + Aetna quote (full-width)
4. Platform in Motion — 3-stage annotated walkthrough
5. Who We Serve — 4 persona tabs (Employers default)
6. How It Works — 3 steps + technical/IP-safe architecture label + crisis protocol callout
7. Trust & Security — data-handling statements + compliance badges
8. Pricing — 3 custom-pricing cards
9. Final CTA — mission framing + Book a Demo

Rationale: credibility front-loaded, evidence dense, mirrors how Abridge and Spring sequence proof. Trust-and-security sits where buyers expect it.

### Arc B — "Show, don't tell" (closest to Hippocratic / Claude)
Best for: a buyer who wants to see the model behave before being told about it.

1. Hero — Chronilogix Marcus chat loop (the demo *is* the hero)
2. Three more de-identified conversation samples (different personas: student, employee, chronic-care patient)
3. Aetna quote + compliance badges
4. Clinical Proof — Resnicow + 3 outcome cards
5. Who We Serve — 4 persona tabs
6. How It Works — 3 steps + IP-safe architecture label
7. Trust & Security
8. Pricing
9. Final CTA

Rationale: leans into the strongest single asset Chronilogix has (MI-grounded conversation). Risk: requires three more polished conversation flows beyond Marcus before launch.

### Arc C — "Problem-first" (closest to Spring Health / Woebot)
Best for: an audience that doesn't already understand the engagement gap behavioral health vendors face.

1. Hero — Chronilogix platform in motion
2. Problem statement — the engagement gap that traditional behavioral health benefit programs leave on the table
3. Aetna quote ("identified and engaged an additional 25%…") as the bridge from problem to Chronilogix
4. Platform in Motion
5. Who We Serve
6. Clinical Proof + Resnicow
7. How It Works + crisis protocol
8. Trust & Security
9. Pricing
10. Final CTA

Rationale: the Aetna stat becomes the inflection point of the page, not a credibility bar. Risk: longer page, more scroll to value.

---

## Recommendation

Arc A is the lowest-risk, highest-credibility choice for a June 2026 healthcare conference launch — it mirrors the sequence Abridge and Spring Health are using to sell into the same buying committees, it lets the existing Aetna anchor and Resnicow assets do most of the work, and it matches the section structure already scoped in CLAUDE.md.

If a second polished conversation flow lands before launch, consider grafting one Hippocratic-style sample into Arc A between sections 2 and 3 — it's the single most distinctive AI-native pattern in the healthcare set and would differentiate Chronilogix from Spring/Lyra without restructuring the page.
